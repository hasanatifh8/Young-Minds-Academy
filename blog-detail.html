10 Smart Study Habits of Board Exam Toppers

September 24, 2026 | 7 min read | Academic Team
Board Exams Revision Time Management

Top scorers aren't always the students who study the longest. They're the ones who study the smartest — with consistent routines, active revision and plenty of practice. Here are ten habits we see again and again in students who do exceptionally well in their board exams.

1. Study at the Same Time Every Day

Toppers rarely rely on motivation — they rely on routine. Fixing a daily study slot, even just two focused hours after school, trains the brain to switch into study mode automatically.

2. Understand First, Memorise Later

Before learning a formula or definition by heart, make sure you understand where it comes from. Concepts you understand are far easier to remember and apply in new questions.

3. Make Your Own Short Notes

Writing compact notes in your own words — with formulas, diagrams and keywords — forces you to process information. These notes become your best revision tool before exams.

4. Revise in Spaced Intervals

Revise a topic one day, one week and one month after learning it. This spaced revision moves information into long-term memory far better than last-minute cramming.

5. Practise With Previous-Year Papers

Board papers repeat patterns. Solving at least the last five years' papers shows you the important topics, question styles and how marks are distributed.

6. Time Yourself

Practise full papers under exam conditions. Timed practice builds speed and helps you plan how long to spend on each section.

7. Learn From Mistakes

Keep a 'mistakes notebook'. Every wrong answer in a test goes in, along with why it was wrong. Reviewing it before exams prevents repeating the same errors.

8. Ask Doubts Immediately

Small doubts become big gaps. Toppers clear doubts the same day — from teachers, classmates or our daily doubt desk.

9. Take Short, Planned Breaks

Study in focused blocks of 40–50 minutes with 5–10 minute breaks. Your brain stays fresh and concentration stays high.

10. Sleep, Eat and Move Well

Seven to eight hours of sleep, regular meals and a little exercise are not distractions from studying — they are what make effective studying possible.

Start Small

You don't need to adopt all ten habits at once. Pick two — say, a fixed study time and a mistakes notebook — and stick with them for three weeks. Then add more.

Good habits, combined with clear teaching and regular feedback, are what turn effort into results. If your child needs help building these routines, our teachers and mentors are here to guide them every step of the way.
